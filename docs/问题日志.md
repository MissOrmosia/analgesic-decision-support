## 一、问题分类体系

### 类别 A｜临床决策与逻辑一致性

指推荐内容自身存在前后矛盾或逻辑断裂的问题。典型形态：上游已判定某类药物禁用（如 NSAIDs），下游推荐中仍出现该类药物，同时又在备注中声明禁用——自相矛盾的输出会严重干扰使用者的判断，甚至造成误用。此类问题的修复原则不是"补充警告"，而是**从推荐源头改写**，保证决策链路单一、自洽。

### 类别 B｜临床安全与风险规避

指涉及患者安全的高危场景识别与处置问题，包括：小儿用药的年龄/剂型分层、NSAIDs 胃肠道禁忌、肾功能不全时的剂量调整与禁用、阿片类药物耐受门槛等。核心设计立场：**高危场景下不鼓励医生自行决策**。AI 的建议不应成为医生放弃寻求专科资源的理由——宁可不提供推荐，也要将医生导向会诊，这是医疗安全与法律风险规避的最优解。

### 类别 C｜审计与依从性管理

指围绕"医生是否遵守规则"的记录与反馈机制。强制会诊场景直接拦截；建议会诊场景允许继续但必须留痕。基于现实考量：忙碌的临床医生有充分动机关闭会诊提示以求方便，因此——

1. **事前告知**：明确提示该行为将被审计记录及其后果；
2. **事中留痕**：拒绝/暂缓会诊须填写通过校验的临床理由，并进行高级别标记；
3. **事后反馈**：提交后给予明确回执。缺乏回执会让遵守规则的医生怀疑系统故障、担心自己"被误记为不守规则"，甚至引发对信息科的不必要咨询——反馈机制本身也是审计公信力的一部分。

### 类别 D｜交互复杂度与认知负荷

指操作流程的繁简问题。目标用户是高度繁忙的临床医生，交互设计以**降低决策负荷、支持快速决策**为第一原则，任何增加点击轮次的设计都需要充分理由。典型权衡：小儿年龄若按 2/6/12 岁分档逐级弹窗，轮次过多，故改为直接输入精确年龄、由系统判断阈值；反之，2 岁以下小儿直接输出镇痛建议本身即属高危，故放弃"便利"选择强制拦截——在医患纠纷高发的儿科诊疗场景中，助手的作用是降低决策负荷，而非将医生导向纠纷。

### 类别 E｜工程实现

指代码缺陷与架构问题，如状态未初始化、子串匹配误判、历史栈快照时序等。此类问题虽为工程问题，但部分会直接传导为临床输出错误（如问题 91），故与类别 A 联动修复。

### 类别 F｜视觉、文案与其他

包括禁忌信息的加粗标红规范、术语全称统一、版本标识，以及开发过程中的代码拼接、文档整理等事务性事项。

## I. Issue Classification System

### Category A | Clinical Decision-Making & Logical Consistency

Issues where the recommendation content itself is internally contradictory or logically broken. Typical form: an upstream decision has already ruled a drug class contraindicated (e.g., NSAIDs), yet the downstream recommendation still contains that class while simultaneously declaring it contraindicated in a note — self-contradictory output seriously interferes with the user's judgment and can cause misuse. The repair principle for such issues is not "add more warnings" but **rewrite at the source of the recommendation**, keeping the decision chain single and self-consistent.

### Category B | Clinical Safety & Risk Mitigation

Issues concerning the identification and handling of high-risk scenarios for patient safety, including: pediatric age/formulation stratification, NSAID gastrointestinal contraindications, dose adjustment and avoidance in renal impairment, and opioid-tolerance thresholds. Core design stance: **physicians should not be encouraged to decide alone in high-risk scenarios**. The AI's advice must not become a reason for the physician to forgo specialist resources — rather than providing a recommendation, the assistant should direct the physician toward a consult. This is the optimal strategy for both medical safety and medico-legal risk mitigation.

### Category C | Audit & Compliance Management

Issues around the recording and feedback mechanisms for "whether the physician follows the rules." Mandatory-consult scenarios block outright; recommended-consult scenarios allow proceeding but must be logged. Based on practical considerations: busy clinicians have every incentive to dismiss consult prompts for convenience. Therefore:

1. **Advance notice**: clearly state that the action will be audit-logged and its consequences;
2. **In-action logging**: declining or deferring a consult requires a validated clinical reason, flagged at a high level;
3. **Post-action feedback**: a clear receipt after submission. Without feedback, rule-abiding physicians suspect a system fault, worry they "were misrecorded as non-compliant," and may even pester IT with unnecessary inquiries — the feedback mechanism is itself part of the audit's credibility.

### Category D | Interaction Complexity & Cognitive Load

Issues of operational flow complexity. The target user is a highly busy clinician; interaction design takes **reducing decision load and supporting rapid decisions** as its first principle, and any design that adds clicks requires strong justification. Typical trade-off: staging pediatric age as 2/6/12-year modals would take too many rounds, so precise age input is used with system-side threshold checks; conversely, printing analgesic recommendations for children under 2 is itself high-risk, so "convenience" is abandoned in favor of mandatory interception — in the litigation-prone setting of pediatric care, the assistant's role is to reduce decision load, not to lead the physician into disputes.

### Category E | Engineering Implementation

Code defects and architecture issues, such as uninitialized state, substring-matching misjudgment, and history-stack snapshot timing. Although engineering in nature, some of these directly propagate into clinical output errors (e.g., Issue 91), so they are fixed in conjunction with Category A.

### Category F | Visuals, Copy & Miscellaneous

Bold-red conventions for contraindication text, full-disease-name terminology, version labels, plus code-concatenation and documentation chores during development.
